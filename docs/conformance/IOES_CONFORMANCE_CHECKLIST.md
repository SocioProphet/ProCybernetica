# IOES Conformance Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist defines the first ProCybernetica conformance surface for IOES-aware systems.

IOES means Identity, Ontogenesis, Ecology, and Stewardship.

A system is IOES-aware only if it can preserve identity continuity, developmental state, ecological dependency context, and stewardship obligations under evidence, policy, replay, and effective-authority constraints.

## Scope

This checklist applies to repositories, agents, bundles, workflows, projections, learning artifacts, graph deltas, policy decisions, and delivery records that may affect humans, communities, ecosystems, knowledge commons, or entrusted artifacts.

## Required declarations

Every IOES-impacting control node must declare:

what it can observe

what it can infer

what it can propose

what it can mutate

what it can export

what authority permits it

what policy gates constrain it

what evidence it emits

how it can be replayed

how it can be revoked, repaired, or superseded

what human-impacting consequences it may trigger

If a component cannot answer these, it is not eligible for human-impacting authority.

## Identity conformance

The system distinguishes humans from accounts, roles, credentials, twins, projections, scores, and model summaries.

Prime-topic boundaries are preserved.

Unsafe merges are blocked or marked as candidate state only.

Identity-affecting claims carry provenance.

Identity state is replayable or linked to replayable evidence.

## Ontogenesis conformance

Developmental state is explicit when used.

Phase transitions preserve history.

Model-inferred developmental state is candidate-only unless promoted by policy and evidence.

Human-impacting developmental changes require authority and review posture.

Blocked transitions preserve reasons and repair paths.

## Ecology conformance

Material dependencies are represented or explicitly marked unknown.

Gaia dependency records preserve evidence and risk posture.

Ecological or community impacts are not stripped from projections or decisions merely for convenience.

Shared-system dependencies identify stewards where possible.

Externalized risk is flagged.

## Stewardship conformance

Stewardship is distinct from ownership.

A steward relation has scope, duties, limits, authority, evidence, and review posture.

A keeper log exists for active stewardship objects.

Succession posture is defined or risk-flagged.

Abandonment is represented as graph state, not hidden absence.

Stewardship transfer requires authority and evidence.

## Consent conformance

Consent is scoped by purpose, subject, duration, projection/export surface, and revocation path.

Consent receipts are linked to relevant graph, twin, projection, learning, execution, or delivery records.

Consent is not silently reused across unrelated purposes.

Revocation is represented and enforced where meaningful.

## Policy conformance

Protected-value vetoes are available for dignity, consent, stewardship, developmental integrity, ecological accountability, provenance, succession, and agent boundedness.

Policy decisions distinguish allow, deny, repair, review, and exception.

Exceptions have authority, rationale, expiry, and review.

Fail-closed behavior is demonstrated for missing consent, authority, evidence, or replayability.

## Evidence conformance

Every canonical claim has evidence or is marked unsupported.

Execution evidence includes validation, placement, run, replay, and policy references where applicable.

Learning promotions include evidence anchors and change records.

Delivery outcomes include proof without reducing humans to productivity objects.

Evidence hashes, refs, and provenance are stable enough for replay and audit.

## Agent conformance

Agents have explicit grants.

Agents do not mutate identity, consent, stewardship, developmental state, canon status, delivery authority, or projection state without scoped permission.

Agent outputs are classified as candidate, proposal, governed mutation, external export, or human-impacting action.

Agent actions emit evidence.

Agent authority can be revoked.

## Projection conformance

Every projection is partial.

Projection-loss profile or equivalent disclosure is present.

Denied, omitted, stale, inferred, and unsupported content is distinguishable.

Projection use is constrained by consent and policy.

A projection does not become the person or entity projected.

## Learning conformance

Learning artifacts cite evidence anchors.

Sandbox and canon states are distinct.

Canon promotion requires governance gate evidence.

Stewardship status is active or risk-flagged.

Mentor, apprentice, or successor posture is represented where relevant.

Learner state is protected from surveillance misuse.

## Delivery conformance

Delivery records distinguish output, outcome, evidence, stewardship, learning, and human-agency posture.

Productivity-only scorecards are rejected for human-development conclusions.

Customer proof does not expose private subject data without consent.

Delivery metrics support repair and learning, not coercive ranking by default.

## Minimum passing tranche

The first IOES conformance fixture should demonstrate:

A stewarded learning artifact.

Evidence anchors.

Policy decision.

Consent receipt where projection occurs.

AgentPlane execution evidence.

Regis graph delta.

Alexandrian Academy change record.

Delivery Excellence outcome record.

Human Digital Twin continuity claim, if consented.

HolographMe projection-loss-aware view, if projected.

A blocked variant showing failure when the artifact lacks active stewardship or policy authority.

## Review posture

Initial status: doctrine-level checklist.

Next status: schema-backed checklist.

Final status: CI-backed conformance lane with valid and invalid fixtures.

## Non-goals

This checklist does not implement runtime enforcement.

It does not certify production readiness.

It does not replace repository-specific validators.

It defines the shared IOES conformance expectations to be made executable by downstream tranches.
